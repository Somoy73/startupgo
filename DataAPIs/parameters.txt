VC STARTUP RECOMMENDATION SYSTEM
COMPREHENSIVE DATA DICTIONARY AND ASSESSMENT PARAMETERS
================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This document lists parameters that can be collected, calculated, and assessed
for a startup recommendation system intended to support venture-capital (VC)
screening and due diligence.

Important distinctions:
- Not every parameter is publicly available.
- Some parameters require founder-submitted information or confidential VC data.
- A measurable parameter is not automatically a reliable predictor of returns.
- Missing data must be distinguished from poor performance.
- Recommendations should be evidence-backed and should communicate uncertainty.
- This framework is a starting point, not a validated predictive model.

================================================================
A. COMPANY IDENTITY AND PROFILE
================================================================
Purpose: Identify the startup, establish context, and enable fair comparisons.

1. Company ID — unique internal identifier
2. Company name — registered and trading name
3. Website — official URL
4. Country of incorporation — legal jurisdiction
5. Headquarters — city and country
6. Date founded — incorporation or operating start date
7. Company age — years/months since founding
8. Industry — primary industry
9. Sub-industry — specific market category
10. Business model — SaaS, marketplace, hardware, services, etc.
11. Customer segment — B2B, B2C, B2G, B2B2C
12. Target geography — markets served or targeted
13. Operating status — active, acquired, inactive, shut down
14. Employee count — current headcount
15. Employee growth — change in headcount over time
16. Company stage — idea, prototype, pre-seed, seed, Series A, etc.
17. Legal structure — corporation, limited company, other
18. Mission and value proposition — what the business promises to deliver
19. Primary problem solved — customer problem being addressed
20. Product category — product or service classification

Assessment:
- Company maturity
- Comparability with other startups
- Sector classification
- Operating history
- Fit with the VC's investment mandate

13. Founder commitment — full-time, part-time, other employment
14. Founder-market fit

16. Vesting arrangements

18. Key-person dependency
19. Hiring capability
20. Employee turnover
21. Advisor network and actual contributions
22. Board composition and governance experience
23. References — verified references and feedback

Assessment:
- Execution capability
- Team gaps
- Key-person risk
- Founder-market fit
- Leadership strength

Caution: Prestige, famous employers, elite universities, or large networks
should not automatically produce high scores. Evaluate relevant performance
and verified evidence.

D. MARKET SIZE AND MARKET OPPORTUNITY
================================================================
1. Total Addressable Market (TAM)
2. Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
3. Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
4. Market growth rate — historical and forecast
5. Market maturity — emerging, growing, mature or declining
6. Target customer count
7. Average customer spending
8. Geographic opportunity — market size by country or region
9. Customer segment distribution
10. Market concentration — dominance of large incumbents
11. Industry fragmentation
12. Market entry barriers — capital, regulatory, technical or distribution
13. Regulatory outlook
14. Technology adoption rate
15. Market timing — evidence that the market is ready now
16. Macro sensitivity — interest rates, economic cycles, etc.
17. Market expansion potential — adjacent sectors or geographies
18. Market growth evidence from independent sources
19. Market estimate methodology — bottom-up, top-down, etc.
20. Realistic revenue ceiling under plausible assumptions

Assessment:
- Whether the market can support venture-scale growth
- Credibility of the startup's market estimates

Prefer bottom-up estimates. Example: 8,000 reachable companies x EUR 25,000
potential annual contract value = EUR 200 million in annual market opportunity.
This is more informative than citing a huge global industry without explaining
the realistically reachable segment.

T. DERIVED PARAMETERS / CALCULATED METRICS
================================================================
These metrics are calculated from raw fields and should have clearly defined
formulas, time periods, units and assumptions.

1. Revenue growth — change in revenue over a defined period
2. Customer growth — change in paying customers over a defined period
3. Gross margin = (Revenue - cost of revenue) / Revenue
4. Net burn = cash outflows - cash inflows
5. Runway = available cash / estimated monthly net burn, when burn is positive
6. CAC = acquisition-related costs / new customers acquired
7. LTV/CAC = estimated customer lifetime value / CAC
8. CAC payback = time needed to recover customer acquisition cost
9. NRR = (starting recurring revenue + expansion - contraction - churn) /
   starting recurring revenue
10. Revenue concentration = revenue from largest customers / total revenue
11. Capital efficiency = growth or revenue achieved relative to capital consumed
12. Hiring growth = percentage change in headcount
13. Market penetration = relevant customers acquired / estimated addressable
    customers
14. Pipeline coverage = qualified pipeline / target bookings or sales
15. Valuation-to-revenue = valuation / revenue, where revenue is meaningful
16. Dilution estimate = ownership impact of proposed and future financing
17. Implied investor MOIC = estimated investor proceeds / total invested capital
18. Scenario-weighted return = returns weighted by explicit scenario probabilities
19. Data completeness = percentage of required fields populated
20. Evidence confidence = reliability, recency and independent verification
21. Risk-adjusted assessment = investment attractiveness after defined risks and
    uncertainty are considered
22. VC thesis fit = alignment with sector, stage, geography and check size

Notes:
- Define all formulas consistently.
- Handle zero denominators and unavailable values explicitly.
- Avoid comparing metrics across incompatible business models or stages.
- Short histories can make LTV, retention and growth estimates unstable.

U. DATA PROVENANCE AND QUALITY PARAMETERS
================================================================
These fields make the recommendation auditable and trustworthy.

1. Source URL or document — origin of each claim
2. Collection timestamp — when retrieved
3. Fact effective date — when the fact was true
4. Verification status — unverified, cross-checked, independently verified
5. Source reliability
6. Confidence score for extracted information
7. Conflicting values from different sources
8. Missingness indicator — distinguish missing from zero
9. Data license — permitted use and redistribution
10. Consent status — authorization for confidential information
11. Update frequency
12. Extraction method — API, document extraction, manual review, etc.
13. Human review status
14. Historical snapshot — what was known at a past decision date
15. Currency and units
16. Metric definition and calculation method

Without these fields, a system can produce convincing recommendations from
outdated, contradictory or unsupported information.

================================================================
W. FINAL VC RECOMMENDATION OUTPUT
================================================================
For each startup, provide:

1. Investment fit
   - How well it matches the VC's strategy and constraints

2. Evidence-based assessment
   - Strengths and weaknesses supported by sourced data

3. Risk analysis
   - Most important risks and unanswered questions

4. Comparable companies
   - Similar startups at a similar stage, with historical outcomes where known

5. Recommended next action
   - Reject, monitor, request information, or advance to due diligence

Avoid presenting a single score such as "87/100 — invest" without context.
Prefer separate dimensions, evidence confidence and explicit reasons to
proceed or stop.

Keep three assessments separate:
- Startup quality
- Quality/completeness of available evidence
- Fit for a specific VC

A strong startup may be a poor fit for one fund. An incomplete profile should
trigger further investigation rather than automatic rejection.

================================================================
X. IMPORTANT MODELING AND DATA-COLLECTION WARNINGS
================================================================
1. Survivorship bias:
   Include failed, acquired, inactive and still-operating startups. Public data
   often overrepresents visible winners.

2. Look-ahead leakage:
   For historical prediction, only use information that was available at the
   actual decision date. Do not use future funding rounds or exits as features
   when predicting what was knowable earlier.

3. Define outcomes:
   Raising another round is not equivalent to being a good investment.
   Define the target precisely, such as follow-on funding, revenue milestones,
   exit outcome or realized investor returns.

4. Missing versus negative:
   Unknown revenue is not zero revenue. Unreported churn is not proof of good
   retention. Preserve missingness explicitly.

5. Stage and sector comparability:
   Compare companies with similar stage, business model, sector, geography and
   time period whenever possible.

6. Evidence quality:
   Distinguish founder claims, public announcements, third-party estimates and
   independently verified metrics.

7. Data licensing and privacy:
   Check API terms and commercial data licenses. Obtain permission for
   confidential financials, pitch decks, internal VC memos and customer data.
   Use access controls and appropriate retention policies.

8. Avoid false precision:
   Scores and probabilities must be validated against relevant outcomes.
   Report uncertainty and assumptions. Do not imply that a score guarantees
   investment success.

9. Bias and fairness:
   Review whether proxies such as school prestige, location, network size or
   past investor backing introduce unfair or irrelevant bias.

10. Human decision support:
    The system should support, not replace, professional investment judgment
    and due diligence.
